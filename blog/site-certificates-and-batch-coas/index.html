Site certificates and batch COAs: read the scope first

Distinguish management-system scope from batch results, match each record to the right identity and keep unanswered document questions explicit.

In this article

  1. Start with the object of the document
  2. Read scope rather than collecting logos
  3. Read the batch result with its product context
  4. Keep a question-to-document record
  5. Leave the application question open until it is evaluated

A management-system certificate and a certificate of analysis can arrive in the same supplier-document folder. They answer different questions. Before filing either, write down what you need to establish: the scope of a supplier’s management system, the reported results for a particular batch, or the suitability of a preparation for your process.

Start with the object of the document

For a management-system certificate such as ISO 9001, read the named organisation, the covered locations and the stated activities. The ISO 9001 Auditing Practices Group explains that certification scope can cover only part of an organisation’s activities or sites. A group brand name alone is therefore not enough to establish coverage. See its guidance on scope and applicability.

A batch COA concerns an identified product and batch. It reports the characteristics tested and their results. Keep that identity with the record; a COA for another product or lot does not answer a question about the material in front of you. SUNSON’s document-request page distinguishes batch records from site certification and product information.

Read scope rather than collecting logos

For the management-system certificate, keep these details together:

  • Entity: the organisation named on the certificate.
  • Location: the relevant site and any referenced site schedule.
  • Activities: the scope wording, including what work it covers.
  • Standard and period: the stated standard, version and certificate dates.
  • Verification: the issuing body and an appropriate way to check status.

Dates on a copy are not a live status check. Where verification is needed, ISO describes checking accredited certification through the relevant certification or accreditation body, or the recognised verification database. Its certification guidance also explains that external bodies perform certification; ISO itself does not certify organisations.

Read the batch result with its product context

For a COA, match the product reference and batch identifier first. Then read the test, result, unit and applicable specification or limit together. If the method is referenced separately, retain that reference. If a result or method detail you need is absent, record the gap and request clarification instead of filling it from a different product sheet.

Distinguish a specification from a batch result. One states a requirement or product characteristic; the other reports information for the identified batch. Neither should silently stand in for the other. Handling and safety questions belong with the corresponding safety information.

Keep a question-to-document record

A practical record to complete with the actual documents
QuestionRecord to inspectIdentity to retain
Which management activities and locations are covered?Management-system certificate and relevant scheduleEntity, site and scope
What was reported for this batch?Relevant batch COAProduct, batch, test and result
What product information or handling guidance is needed?Corresponding technical or safety documentExact product reference and document identity

Alongside each row, keep the question still unanswered and the clarification requested. This separates missing information from evidence that actually contradicts a requirement.

Leave the application question open until it is evaluated

ISO’s supply-chain guidance distinguishes quality-management conformity from product conformity. A management-system certificate is not a certificate for every product. A batch result, in turn, does not establish how a preparation will perform under every formulation or processing condition.

For SUNSON records, name the company or site for certification questions, and the product and batch for batch enquiries. Explain the document’s intended use. Batch COAs are controlled records handled through the appropriate customer or order contact. Use the manufacturing and quality page for company context, then request the specific evidence needed.
