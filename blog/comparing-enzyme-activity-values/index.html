Measurement & application

Before comparing two enzyme activity values

Check the assay definition, conditions and reporting basis before comparing enzyme activity values, then separate the assay decision from the application trial.

In this article

  1. Read the measurement definition first
  2. Keep the reporting basis attached
  3. Build a short comparison record
  4. Choose the next step from the mismatch
  5. Keep the process result separate

Two enzyme preparations can carry activity values that look easy to rank. The useful first question is whether those values describe the same measurement. Before choosing the larger number or calculating a replacement quantity, put the method, conditions and reporting basis beside each result.

Read the measurement definition first

The IUBMB’s enzyme-activity terminology ties catalytic activity to a specified reaction in a specified assay system. The enzyme name and an activity abbreviation do not supply all that information. Ask what reaction was measured, how the signal was detected and which method defines the reported unit.

A published protease assay procedure, for example, defines its measurement through a tyrosine-equivalent readout under its described conditions. That illustrates why the method matters; it is not a statement that SUNSON preparations use that procedure, or a recommendation to apply it to every protease.

Keep the reporting basis attached

Read the denominator as carefully as the activity symbol. Activity per gram of a preparation, per millilitre of liquid and per milligram of protein describe different reporting bases. “Per milligram” also needs its subject: protein and formulated product are not interchangeable labels.

The BRENDA data-field definitions specify a protein-mass basis for specific activity and retain comments about assay conditions and substrates. When reviewing commercial documents, ask what mass or volume the reported value refers to. Do not infer that basis from whether the supplied preparation looks like a powder or a liquid.

Build a short comparison record

The STRENDA guidelines describe information needed to report enzymology experiments, including enzyme identity, the method and assay conditions. They are research-reporting guidance, not a product-certification scheme. The following compact record adapts that emphasis on context for a document discussion.

Copy the actual details for each preparation; leave unknowns explicit
Keep with each valueWhat to transcribe or ask
PreparationProduct and batch identity; which enzyme activity is reported
DefinitionExact unit definition and the mass or volume basis
MethodMethod reference, version and any stated modification
Reaction and readoutSubstrate, measured signal and calculation reference
ConditionsReported pH, temperature, buffer, concentrations and timing
Document trailSource document, relevant page and the clarification still needed

Make a separate record for each product. Preserve the original wording before adding your interpretation. A missing method reference is an unanswered question, not proof that the value is wrong.

Choose the next step from the mismatch

  • The definitions and conditions align: a direct numerical comparison may be meaningful. Confirm that the reporting bases and the decision being made also align.
  • Information is missing: request the definition or method summary before ranking the values.
  • The methods differ: retain both original results and discuss whether comparison under a common method is appropriate. Changing the printed unit alone does not resolve a difference in what was measured.

Keep the process result separate

A comparable assay value still answers an assay question. It does not by itself establish the result in a particular material or formulation. SUNSON’s detergent protease information distinguishes activity during storage from performance in a finished-formulation wash test. These are different measurements, each with its own conditions.

Define the application objective and retain the conditions with the observations. For a specific SUNSON reference, use the technical documents page to identify the appropriate product or batch information. Bring the original values and their method context into that discussion, rather than relying on a universal conversion factor or an assumed application dose.

  • BRENDA: Description of data fields, specific activity
  • Sigma-Aldrich: Assay Procedure for Protease
