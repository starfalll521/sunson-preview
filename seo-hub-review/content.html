Detergent formulation

Explore detergent enzymes

Start with the stain you need to remove. The enzyme has to work with the detergent formulation, product format and intended washing conditions. Those details provide the starting point for evaluating cleaning performance and resource-use goals.

Protein-containing stains

Protease acts on protein-containing material. A detergent protease belongs to a cleaning application, with requirements different from those of a feed protease.

Protease for detergents

Enzyme combinations in a formulation

Multi-enzyme preparations combine functions for a particular formulation task. The intended liquid or powder system, incorporation and storage conditions inform product selection.

Evaluate detergent enzyme blends

The same enzyme name can mean different application choices.

A shared enzyme name does not make preparations interchangeable. The intended use, formulation and performance requirements belong to the application.

Protease in animal feed

The material is dietary protein. The application concerns protein digestion in the relevant animal and diet.

SFP-MUL02 feed protease

Protease in a detergent

The material is a protein-containing stain. The application concerns cleaning within a detergent system.

Detergent protease applications

Start with the material and the task.

Enzyme choice becomes clearer when the material, the stage of use and the intended result are kept distinct. The relevant product information then adds the conditions for a particular preparation.

What material is involved?
Feed ingredients, fruit mash or extracted juice, and stains within a detergent formulation pose different questions.
Where does the action belong?
Dietary use differs from feed processing. Fruit treatment before pressing differs from treatment before clarification.
What result matters?
Phosphorus use is not retained activity after heat treatment. Juice clarity is not juice recovery. Storage activity is not final cleaning performance.
