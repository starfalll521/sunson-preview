How enzymes create value in industrial processes.

Industrial enzymes are the starting point for SUNSON's biosolutions. Their specific reactions can change how materials behave during processing or how a product performs in use.

Our purpose is healthier lives and a healthier planet. Follow an apple from fruit to juice to see how enzyme choice depends on your material, process and the result you need.

Specific reactions. Practical biosolutions.

The substance involved in an enzyme-catalysed reaction is called its substrate. An enzyme acts on particular bonds or structures within that substrate. The resulting change can alter properties that matter in processing, such as the size of molecules or the way a material separates.

This selectivity explains why enzyme choice starts with the material. A protease cannot replace a pectin-degrading activity simply because both are enzymes. The enzyme also has to reach the reaction site; the structure of the material and the way it is mixed can affect that contact.

Different materials. Different reactions.

Pectin is a component of fruit tissue. Pectin-degrading activities change its structure, which can affect viscosity and processing behaviour. In other settings, proteases act on proteins in stains, while phytase acts on phytate in plant-based feed ingredients. Each reaction addresses a different material question.

Enzyme and preparation.

An enzyme is the catalyst. An industrial enzyme preparation is the product used in a process and may contain several enzyme activities, carriers or stabilising ingredients. The enzyme name alone does not describe the complete preparation.

From fruit to juice. Two process goals.

Making apple juice involves more than changing the fruit into liquid. Crushing produces a mash. Pressing separates juice from that mash. Further treatment prepares the extracted juice for clarification and filtration. Pectin can be relevant at more than one point along this route.

Before pressing: the mash.

Before pressing, treatment acts on the fruit mash. The question concerns how that material releases juice and behaves during pressing. Pectin-related treatment is therefore considered in the context of juice recovery from a defined amount of fruit.

Explore mash treatment and recovery

After extraction: the juice.

After extraction, the juice can still contain pectin. Treatment of residual pectin, often called depectinization, addresses a later question: how the extracted juice will behave during clarification or filtration. The material being treated and the next processing step have both changed.

Explore clarification and filtration

Pectinase describes a group of activities rather than one interchangeable product. Some activities break bonds along the pectin chain; others change its ester groups. The combination of activities and the structure of the pectin help explain why preparations can behave differently.

Enzyme benefits need a useful measure.

The value of an enzyme-based biosolution depends on the result you need. In juice processing, recovery and clarity are separate measures. Compare treatments against a defined material and reference, with a consistent measurement basis.

The intended product matters too. Clear juice, fruit purée and fruit pieces have different appearance and texture goals. Greater breakdown is not a shared target for all of them. A useful reaction serves the product and the processing task.

Enzyme solutions depend on the process.

An enzyme preparation has to work within your process. Temperature, pH, contact time and mixing affect the reaction. Goals such as lower resource use need their own measurements and a suitable comparison.

Being inside a stated temperature range does not establish that a treatment will reach its target within your available time. A product's stated range, its activity assay conditions and its performance in a particular process answer different questions.

From research to a usable preparation.

At SUNSON, strain research and application research connect the biological source of an enzyme with the work a preparation is intended to do. Manufacturing operations in Yinchuan and Cangzhou connect that development with production.

Preparation form is part of this work. Our detergent range includes liquid and granular preparations and multi-enzyme blends. The choice needs to suit the detergent system alongside the enzyme function.
