Enzymes for
alcohol production.

A useful fermentation starts with the feedstock. Explore enzymes for liquefaction and saccharification of starch-based materials, then match the preparation to the conditions in your process.

Explore alcohol enzymes

Start with the starch.

Corn, cassava and wheat bring different processing conditions. In liquefaction, alpha-amylase breaks starch chains into shorter dextrins. The enzyme choice belongs alongside the slurry preparation, solids content and heating profile.

SUNSON FDY-3002 is a thermostable alpha-amylase for starch liquefaction in alcohol production. Review its current instructions against the proposed addition point and residence time.

Prepare sugars for fermentation.

Glucoamylase releases glucose from starch-derived chains during saccharification. SUNSON FDY-3003 connects this enzyme function with the starch-conversion stage in alcohol production.

Saccharification and fermentation may be separate or combined in a process. Record the actual enzyme and yeast addition points, pH, temperature and timing. Yeast carries out the fermentation of sugar to ethanol; the enzyme’s role is to make the substrate available.

Does your material raise another question?

Other enzyme functions may be relevant to the feedstock. SUNSON FDY-3001 xylanase addresses wheat-based processing, while FDG-3004 acid protease hydrolyses proteins. Discuss the material and purpose before choosing an additional enzyme.

Measure each stage on its own terms.

An enzyme activity value, a sugar result and an ethanol result answer different questions. Keep the method and calculation basis beside each observation.

Define the input

Record feedstock, starch or solids basis, batch size, water additions and preparation.

Track conversion

Follow fermentation

Record yeast, pH, temperature and time. State the method and basis used to report ethanol concentration or recovery.

The end use matters. Discuss whether the process makes potable, industrial or fuel alcohol and which product documentation applies. No yield, energy-saving or environmental result is promised by this overview.
