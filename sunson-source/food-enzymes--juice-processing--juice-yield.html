Enzymes for juice yield and recovery

In fruit processing, treatment before pressing targets the material entering extraction. Pectinase belongs in a discussion of fruit-mash treatment and the recovery of juice from that material.

Treatment upstream of pressing

Raw-material condition and the pressing process shape the application. The available treatment time and the point of addition need to fit the actual production sequence.

Pectinase for juice processing · SAD-016

Juice recovery is a material-balance question

An increase in recovered liquid must be defined against the incoming fruit material and the selected measurement basis. A change in dilution or solids content should remain visible when interpreting the result.

Separate yield from clarity

Juice recovery and clarity can both matter to a processor, but they are not interchangeable. Extracted-juice treatment and separation are described on the clarification page.

Juice clarification

Define juice yield before calculating a change

For a mass-based comparison, one possible reporting basis is:

Collected juice yield (%) = collected juice mass ÷ defined input material mass × 100

State the input basis and the collection endpoint with the result. Record water and other additions separately; their mass must not be mistaken for extra juice recovered from the fruit.

If your process uses a different yield definition, agree on that definition before testing and apply it consistently. When measuring volume, document the conversion to mass if the results are reported on a mass basis.

Reporting a difference

The percentage-point change is treated yield minus reference yield. The relative change is that difference divided by reference yield, multiplied by 100. A relative change is only defined when the reference yield is greater than zero.

State which calculation is used. For example, a change from 60% to 63% is an increase of 3 percentage points, or a 5% relative increase.
